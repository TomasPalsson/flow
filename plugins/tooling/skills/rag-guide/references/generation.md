# Generation, Grounding & Citations

Confidence tiers used below: **unmarked** = traced to a primary source (vendor doc or paper text fetched Aug 2026); **"reported"/"≈"** = one source, usually an abstract, un-reproduced; **"unverified"** = named as such, do not build on it. Two independent research passes ran this thread; where they disagreed, that is stated rather than averaged.

## Prompt architecture — position is a measured lever, not taste

Anthropic's own [prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) ("Long context prompting"), scoped to **20k+ token inputs**: put long documents *above* the query, instructions, and examples — "Queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests, especially with complex, multidocument inputs." Read the hedges honestly: "up to," "in tests," no benchmark named. It is an upper bound from unpublished internal evals, and it is still the strongest documented vendor position on the question — and it contradicts the instructions-first habit carried over from short-prompt work. Independent corroboration, reported: KohakuRAG (arXiv:2603.07612), 1st on both WattBot 2025 leaderboards, found **prompt ordering its largest single ablation term (+80% relative)** — larger than its entire BM25 component (+3.1pp).

Documented XML shape: `<documents>` wrapping `<document index="n">` with `<source>` and `<document_content>` subtags. The `index` is the hook for hand-rolled citation IDs. **NEVER mix Anthropic's documented 1-indexed `<document index="1">` with the Citations API's 0-indexed `document_index`** — WHY: the off-by-one emits citations pointing at the *adjacent* document, which resolve, are topically related, and are wrong. It is indistinguishable from a model attribution failure and gets misdiagnosed as one. Assert the convention in a test.

Put **source authority, publication date, and freshness into per-document metadata** — the model has no internal sense of which of your chunks is newer, and this is the only channel by which a recency or authority preference can be made available to it.

**Quote-before-answer** (`<quotes>` then the answer) is the documented cheap alternative to a citations API: "This helps Claude focus on the relevant content and ignore the rest of the document." It constrains the *reasoning*, not just the annotation. But **NEVER run quote-extraction prompting and the Citations API together** — WHY: `cited_text` is free on output tokens while your extracted quotes are not; you pay twice for one guarantee.

## Distractors: the near-miss is the harmful class

The chunk that hurts is not the irrelevant one — it is the *almost*-relevant one a similarity reranker promotes precisely because it is semantically close. This indicts naive top-k-by-similarity: the reranker's objective function is a distractor-generator.

- **Magnitude status:** both research passes reach the qualitative finding; neither traced a number. The commonly-repeated "random noise improves accuracy up to ~35%" (attributed to Cuconasu et al., *The Power of Noise*, SIGIR 2024, arXiv:2401.14887) came only from a secondary citation, and the second pass could not re-verify the paper at all. **Treat the magnitude as unverified. Quote the direction, never the number.**
- Corroborating from the abstention side (reported): swapping random distractors for **BM25 hard negatives dropped correct refusal 99.3% → 70.7%** in a controlled SQuAD 2.0 audit, while random distractors did not move it.
- **Context dilution**, the cleanest controlled measurement (reported, arXiv:2603.15723): expand context with distractors while holding the answer span intact — accuracy falls consistently, and **multi-hop degrades ≈2× as fast as single-span extraction** (HotpotQA vs SQuAD). Consequence: **multi-hop RAG should run a *tighter* k than single-hop over the same corpus**, the opposite of the usual instinct.
- **The "Relevance–Robustness Gap"** (reported, arXiv:2605.01302): maximizing query-relevance preferentially surfaces *sycophantic* evidence that confirms the question's presupposition. A generation failure caused by a retrieval objective — which is why "improve recall" can make groundedness worse.
- **Your eval understates this.** CUB (reported, arXiv:2505.16518): context-utilisation methods "display inflated performance on simple synthesised datasets." **NEVER build a distractor eval from random Wikipedia paragraphs** — WHY: random negatives are the harmless class, so your groundedness numbers come back optimistic on the exact failure mode you shipped.

## Abstention, honestly

**Start from the baseline that reframes everything below: adding retrieval *destroys* abstention.** On questions a model should decline, willingness to decline collapses once context is supplied — **100% → 18.6%** (Gemini 1.5 Pro) and **84.1% → 52%** (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) ([arXiv:2411.06037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037), ICLR 2025). With context present but *insufficient*, frontier models hallucinate at rates rivalling their abstention rates (Gemini 1.5 Pro: 40.4% hallucinate vs 50.0% abstain). Retrieval does not make a model more careful — it makes it more confident, which is the reverse of what the grounding story predicts. That is the hole the rest of this section is trying to plug.

The popular claim ("add an abstention instruction, get correct refusals") is mostly a **measurement artifact**. Controlled audit (reported; Qwen2.5-Instruct 1.5B/3B/7B, SQuAD 2.0, 8,700 generations, [rag-abstention-audit](https://github.com/asohotoo190401/rag-abstention-audit)): a keyword-based refusal detector showed 69.1% → 99.3%; after fixing the detector, the *unaided* model already refused correctly **97.8%** of the time and the instruction's remaining lift to 99.8% was **not significant (p ≥ 0.13)**.

| Finding | What it means operationally |
|---|---|
| Instruction changes wording, not behavior: 440/450 declines without it (never using the literal phrase), 447/450 verbatim with it; median refusal 33 → 8 words | For capable models, budget it as a formatting control, not a safety control |
| At **1.5B: 42.0% → 67.3%** correct refusal; at 7B: nothing | Genuinely load-bearing for small/cheap generators — do not delete it there |
| Four paraphrases of the same instruction → false-refusal 2.7%–14.0% | The exact wording is not a minor detail; version and regression-test it |
| Hard negatives: 68.0% off vs 70.7% on | **Nothing prompt-side fixes near-miss distractors.** This needs a sufficiency gate |

**The gate is the real fix.** Joren et al., *Sufficient Context* (ICLR 2025, Google Research): strong models "excel at answering queries when the context is sufficient, but often output incorrect answers instead of abstaining when the context is not" — capability buys *confident* wrong answers, not abstention. Selective generation via a sufficiency classifier improved correct-answer share **2–10%** across Gemini/GPT/Gemma. Stronger still (reported, arXiv:2605.14115): **conflict-aware abstention beats confidence-only selective accuracy by 7.2–33.4 points**. Abstaining on low confidence is the weak version; abstaining on *detected evidence conflict* is the strong one. Prompt-only abstainers are Pareto-dominated by a four-way trust-parametric / trust-context / trust-either / abstain decision (reported, SABER, arXiv:2605.18792).

Two counterweights, so you do not over-steer. **Over-refusal is a real symptom too**: "when all retrieved documents are irrelevant, RALMs still tend to refuse questions they could have answered correctly" (reported, arXiv:2509.01476, AAAI 2026) — refusal rate and calibration are different things. And **report coverage or report nothing**: "92% accurate" without a coverage figure is unfalsifiable. Vectara's leaderboard **excludes refusals from scoring**, so the field's most-quoted faithfulness metric awards exactly zero credit for correct abstention — read the answer-rate column (Phi-4 3.7% at **80.7%** answer rate; snowflake-arctic 4.3% at **62.7%**) before concluding a model is faithful. Reported: before comparing any two hallucination numbers, ask how each classified a refusal — correct / incorrect / excluded are three defensible, mutually incompatible conventions that move headlines by tens of points.

## Citations in production

[Anthropic Citations API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/build-with-claude/citations) (all active models; text only, no image citations). Note `docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/...` now 301-redirects to `platform.claude.com/docs/en/...` — update hardcoded links.

| Property | Value | Why it bites |
|---|---|---|
| Granularity | plain text → sentence + char range; PDF → sentence + page; **custom content → no further chunking**, block indices | Put each RAG chunk in its own plain-text document for *within*-chunk sentence citation; use custom content when the chunk is the atomic citable unit |
| Enablement | **all-or-none per request** | You cannot mark some sources citable and others not |
| Structured outputs | **400 error** — citations interleave blocks, incompatible with strict JSON schema | "Cited answer, returned as strict JSON" is currently unsatisfiable in one call. Architect for two calls *before* building |
| Cost | `cited_text` counts toward **neither** output tokens nor input tokens when passed back | The "citations will blow up my output bill" objection is wrong here. Documents cache via `cache_control`; citation blocks do not |
| `document_index` | 0-indexed **across all document blocks in the request, spanning all messages** | Rebuilding that list between turns (dropping stale docs, re-ranking) silently reindexes every citation you already stored. **Persist resolved document identity, never the index** |

`search_result` content blocks are the more RAG-native primitive — schema `{type, source, title, content:[{type:"text",text}], citations:{enabled}}`, delivered from a tool call (dynamic) or as top-level user content (pre-fetched); reported to work with no special prompting and to map ~1:1 onto `(source, title, text)` chunk-store output. All active models except Claude Haiku 3. Reported from one pass only: **Cohere** grounded generation is also generation-time and span-level but indexes in the **opposite direction** — `start`/`end` offsets into the *generated* text pointing out to `DocumentSource` objects, so one span can cite several documents; Anthropic indexes into the *source*. The second pass did not re-fetch Cohere or Vertex — treat both vendors' current API shape as unverified.

**Generation-time is not strictly better.** The only head-to-head found (reported, arXiv:2509.21557): post-hoc citation favors **coverage**, generation-time favors **precision at the cost of coverage and speed**, and **retrieval quality dominates both** — attribution mostly inherits your retriever's problems. If compliance says "every sentence carries a source," a post-hoc pass wins. Reported: a post-hoc corrector (CiteFix, arXiv:2504.15629) gave +15.46% relative citation accuracy — enough to swap in a generator ~12× cheaper and 3× faster. A cheap verifier can be net-negative cost.

**The central failure: a valid pointer is not entailment.** Anthropic and Cohere both guarantee the citation resolves into a real document — neither guarantees the span *supports* the claim. Named and measured (all reported): *Deceptive Grounding* (arXiv:2607.09349) — real, resolvable, topically-relevant citation describing the **wrong entity**, explicitly "invisible to faithfulness, hallucination, and citation checks"; **7.8% in a live 740-drug–disease production system, 13.6% for recently approved drugs**, up to 86.7% adversarially in *domain-fine-tuned* models (fine-tuning made it worse); a dedicated entity-attribution verifier hits 97.0% precision / 98.7% recall. *ProvenAI* (arXiv:2606.26449) names the **citation-influence gap**: 53.53% answer accuracy vs 71.55% citation fidelity, with cited sources having weak influence while uncited ones shifted the output. *Cited but Not Verified* (arXiv:2605.06635): frontier models reach >94% link validity and >80% relevance but only **39–77% factual accuracy**, and fact-check accuracy fell ≈42% as tool calls scaled 2 → 150 — link-validity dashboards stay green the whole way down.

**Defense ladder, cost order:** (1) span exists verbatim in the cited doc — catches fabrication only, and **most teams stop here, which is the mistake**; (2) entity-consistency check between claim and span; (3) NLI entailment claim ⊨ span; (4) leave-one-out influence testing — the only rung that closes the influence gap. **Document-level citation is theater** — verifying it costs the user the same effort as answering the question. Sentence/char-range is the granularity floor.

**NEVER measure attribution at the retriever.** WHY (reported, arXiv:2608.00705): retrieved-set precision 0.12–0.23 vs actual citation precision 0.48–0.65, and substituting one for the other **inverts the architecture ranking** — you pick the wrong system. Same paper, for anyone using an LLM judge as the groundedness gate: self-κ **0.137 across embedders (41% verdict change)** and κ ≤ 0.14 re-judging *frozen* inputs eleven weeks later, against a same-day floor of 0.76. Pin versions, keep a frozen regression set, and prefer a small specialized detector for the gate (reported: a 0.6B token-level detector beat QwQ-32B, arXiv:2605.12384; a millisecond-latency T3+OCSVM detector cut false positives 44–55pp while "14B LLM judges suffer latency and calibration problems," arXiv:2605.17034). Judge methodology itself → **`agent-evals`**.

## Hallucination despite correct context

"If the answer is in the context, the model will use it" is false often enough to be a named research area: **context-memory conflict** (arXiv:2404.16032; ReDeEP arXiv:2410.11414; LUMINA; Astute RAG, ACL 2025 — the last framing imperfect retrieval as a *post-retrieval* problem the generator must reconcile, not assume away).

- **The Override Gap** (reported, arXiv:2604.23750): sorting 194 conflicts by *prior strength*, accuracy drops **68% → 16%**. Diagnosed as "a magnitude, not representation, problem" — injected-knowledge margin is roughly constant while pretrained margins grow with fact frequency. **Operational consequence: your RAG fails hardest on exactly the entities users ask about most.** Test with the head of your query distribution; tail-only testing systematically under-samples the failure.
- Baseline compliance is worse than most people assume (reported, TokenMem arXiv:2607.22625): vanilla RAG followed the provided document only **20–52%** of the time when it contradicted priors, across five models.
- **The opposite failure runs simultaneously** (reported, arXiv:2604.22193; 27 models): models weight document assertions above user assertions and are largely "impressionable," failing to separate helpful from harmful external claims — and **post-training strengthens this**. So "just tell it to only use the provided context" is not a fix; it amplifies whichever failure your corpus contains. Anything that can write to your index can write the answer.
- **NEVER read the chain-of-thought as evidence the answer is grounded.** WHY (reported, arXiv:2605.27773): under knowledge conflict, CoT is nearly identical whether the model follows the document or its own memory — flip pairs retained **96%** same-answer similarity. The trace is not an audit trail. Same mechanism as the reasoning-model issue below: **the thinking block is ungrounded, self-authored text that then enters the context the answer conditions on** — functionally an unretrieved document with no provenance that the model implicitly trusts.

## Reasoning models — per model, not per category

Verified against the [Vectara HHEM leaderboard](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard) (HHEM-2.3, header "Last updated on May 11, 2026", grepped raw). Same-family same-snapshot pairs — the only fair comparison — give a **consistent direction and a small magnitude: +0.0 to +2.4pp** (gpt-5.2 low 8.4% → high 10.8%; gpt-5.1 10.9 → 12.1; gpt-5 minimal 14.7 → high 15.1; grok-4.1-fast 17.8 → 19.2; grok-4-fast 19.7 → 20.2; o4-mini 18.6 → 18.6). Reasoning models occupy **both extremes**: worst on the board is `o3-pro` at **23.3%**, while `gpt-5.2-low` at **8.4%** beats gpt-4o (9.6%), claude-haiku-4-5 (9.8%) and every Claude 4.x row (10.3%–12.2%), and `qwen3-next-80b-a3b-thinking` (9.3%) beats most non-thinking peers. A blanket ban pushes you toward *worse* faithfulness.

Three confounds before you use any of these numbers. **Length**: every effort pair that got worse also got longer (gpt-5.1 165.5 → 254.4 words) and the metric flags a summary if *any* claim is unsupported; the one pair with no length increase (o4-mini) is the one pair with zero delta — n=1, but the right shape. **Task**: this is single-document *summarization* faithfulness — no multi-chunk retrieval, no distractors, no conflicting sources, no abstention, no citations. Extrapolating leaderboard rank to multi-doc RAG QA is an inference the authors never make; Vectara says outright they "do not recommend using this as a standalone metric," and concedes verbatim copying would score perfectly (the board rewards extractiveness, which is why nano/lite models top it). **Snapshots**: both the HHEM version and the private ~7,700-article corpus change, so **cross-snapshot comparison is invalid** — this is exactly what produced the widely-circulated "R1 14.3% / V3 3.9%" pair, which appears **nowhere** in the current file (current: **11.3% vs 6.1%**) and could not be traced to any reachable primary source. Debunked as stated; possibly true under an early-2025 HHEM-2.1 snapshot, unconfirmed.

**The replacement rule:** select by measured faithfulness on your own data, not by class. If you use a reasoning model for grounded answering — turn effort to low/minimal (usually the faithfulness-optimal point), cap output length explicitly, force a quote or citation pass over the *documents* so the trace is not the answer's only evidence, and never quote a leaderboard number without its snapshot date. Reasoning models earn their keep in RAG on **query planning, multi-hop decomposition, whether-to-retrieve, and conflict arbitration** — search problems, not grounding problems; plan with the reasoner, write the grounded answer with a cheap faithful model (an architectural inference, unverified as a benchmarked result). A separate, better-founded worry does exist: reasoning-enhancement training causally amplifies **tool-call** hallucination in agentic settings (*The Reasoning Trap*, ACL 2026, arXiv:2510.22977) — a different failure mode; do not conflate it with the HHEM summarization numbers.

## Conflicting documents

**Order sensitivity is the headline.** Reported (arXiv:2605.14115, six open-weight models, HealthContradict): **reversing document order flips 11.4%–25.2% of predictions.** With contradictory sources and no explicit conflict handling, up to a quarter of your answers are decided by your reranker's tie-breaking — and any reranker A/B on a conflicted corpus is measuring order effects as quality. Evaluate at multiple orders, deliberately.

Most real conflict is **obsolescence, not adversarial contradiction** — old versions of the same document that were never deleted (reported motivating figure: >30,000 changed CVEs among 200,000+ in a decade, arXiv:2604.14172). Models also handle source-vs-source worse than source-vs-memory: pitting text against knowledge-graph evidence, LLMs "frequently fail to pick the reliable source" and **default to a blanket modality preference** rather than adjudicating per claim (reported, ConflictQA arXiv:2604.11209).

What to do: **detect conflict as a pipeline stage before generation** — a two-stage detector (embedding MLP, escalate only ambiguous pairs to an LLM) reported **62% API cost cut at 90.8% detection accuracy / 88.7% F1**, with credibility-based source selection +7.1% over manual heuristics (ConflictRAG arXiv:2605.17301) — and wire the detector's output into abstention, since conflict-aware abstention is the strong variant. Then **prompt for an explicit conflict map**: have the model surface contradictory claims and attribute each position before synthesizing. A silently merged answer over contradictory sources is unfalsifiable by the user; two attributed positions are checkable. (Same principle as quote-first: force a visible intermediate artifact so a wrong resolution is inspectable. No direct A/B measurement of "surface the conflict" prompting was found — this is an inference from the order-flip result.)

**NEVER make recency the default tiebreak.** WHY: it is right for a policy document that got updated and wrong for legal precedent, where an older higher-authority source should win — a fixed default silently produces wrong resolutions in those domains. Make it configurable, put effective dates in per-document metadata, and state the rule *in the prompt* ("when sources disagree, prefer the later effective date, and say so"). Recency can never be inferred; the model has no channel for it but yours.

## Conversational RAG

Two independent 2026 lines converge on **abstention, not retrieval, as the multi-turn bottleneck** (both reported): the SemEval-2026 Task 8 Task-A winner states "answerability calibration, not retrieval coverage, is the main end-to-end bottleneck" (arXiv:2603.10524), and K-FinHallu finds justified abstention "the weakest axis across all evaluated models" (arXiv:2605.29523). Corroborating that retrieval rank ≠ end-to-end rank: one system placed 3rd/38 on retrieval and 15th/29 end-to-end (arXiv:2606.28352).

**Treat the rewrite as an additional query, not a substitute.** The competitive systems all hedge — dual-query merged retrieval retaining the original, "controlled" rewriting, full-history conditioning at rewrite/rerank/generate — and the winner used **query diversity over retriever diversity** (5 complementary reformulations into one corpus-aligned sparse retriever, variance-aware nested RRF), explicitly claiming that beats heterogeneous retriever ensembling (all reported). WHY it matters: a rewrite is lossy compression of intent, and the loss is asymmetric — a rewrite that drops a constraint retrieves plausible-but-wrong evidence which the generator then grounds on *faithfully*. **Faithful grounding on the wrong evidence is invisible to every groundedness metric in this file.**

**Re-retrieve vs reuse.** Reuse when the follow-up is a *transformation* of the current answer (summarize, translate, reformat, elaborate). Re-retrieve when it introduces a new entity, a new constraint, or a time reference. Make the decision with a component that emits **what is missing**, not a boolean: S2G-RAG's per-turn sufficiency judge emits structured gap items that map into the next query (reported, arXiv:2604.23783, ACL 2026) — and that specification is exactly what a good abstention message needs ("I have X but not Y"), so **the re-retrieval controller and the abstention controller are the same component**; building them separately is duplicated work. Related reported mechanisms: binary routing classifier (RoTRAG, ~40% avg relative F1 gain), hidden-state probing gate with a typed repair router and an explicit *exit skill* for irreducible cases (Skill-RAG arXiv:2604.15771 — the exit skill is the abstention hook most systems omit), conformal per-turn error budgets for adaptive stopping (MiCP arXiv:2604.01413). Counterintuitive and reported: **better state tracking reduces retrieval rounds** — 78.7% vs 41.3% trajectory success while cutting average turns 8.4 → 3.9 (DQA arXiv:2604.05350).

Two decay effects to design against. **Redundancy**: byte-exact dedup measured 0.16% on clean BeIR passages, 24.03% on enterprise patterns, and **80.34% in multi-turn conversational AI** (reported, arXiv:2605.09611) — and near-duplicates are not behaviorally neutral, they vote-stuff whichever source your corpus happens to contain twice. Dedup accumulated context aggressively. **Self-authored drift**: as history grows, original chunks compete with many turns of *model-generated* text that the model treats as equally authoritative context — periodically re-inject the source documents near the end of the prompt. Also reported: framing flips produce contradictory conclusions on identical evidence, "further amplified in multi-turn conversations, where sustained persuasion increases inconsistency" (arXiv:2604.05051).
