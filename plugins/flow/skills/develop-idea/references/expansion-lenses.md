# Expansion Lenses — the "have you thought of X?" engine

Every idea is a bundle of assumptions. Your job is to find the **load-bearing** ones and the **conspicuously absent** ones. Do NOT ask all of these. Find the 2-3 dimensions missing from *this* idea, go deep, skip what the user has clearly already worked through.

## The 13 dimensions

**1. Problem** *(startups, features, strategy, research)* — vitamin or painkiller?
- Who feels this pain *today*? Name a specific person, not a demographic.
- How do they cope now, and why is the workaround insufficient? (The workaround reveals what they value.)
- Is the problem growing, stable, or shrinking? A real problem in a declining context is a trap.

**2. User / Audience** *(all types — the most universally underexplored)*
- What's the most *specific* possible description of the primary user? ("38-yr-old eng manager at a 200-person SaaS who runs 1:1s in Notion," not "busy professionals.")
- Who's the non-obvious user who'll adopt this in a way you didn't predict?
- Who's the decision-maker vs. the user vs. the payer? Often three different people.

**3. Desirability / Demand** *(startups, features, essays)*
- What's the *evidence* of demand — someone paying, signing up, changing behavior — not "that sounds cool"?
- What's their revealed preference: what are they doing with time/money *right now* that shows what they value?
- What would they have to give up (time, money, habit, status) to adopt this?

**4. Feasibility** *(startups, features, research, strategy)*
- What's the *single hardest thing* that has to work? Not a list — the one thing.
- What capability/resource/permission do you not have and would need to get?
- Who's done the closest thing before, and what did it take them?

**5. Viability / Business model** *(startups, resource-constrained strategy)*
- How does this sustain itself? Name the mechanism (revenue, funding, institutional, volunteer).
- What's the distribution strategy? (Distribution is a feasibility question, not an afterthought.)
- **Why now?** What changed in the world that makes this the right moment? (Often the most revealing question.)
- What's the moat a well-resourced copycat can't cross?

**6. Differentiation** *(all types)*
- **What does the user do if this doesn't exist?** The "do nothing" alternative is chronically underestimated.
- What are the *indirect* substitutes — a totally different approach to the same need?
- What's the core differentiating insight — the thing only this idea believes that others don't?

**7. Risks & failure modes** *(all types)*
- What's the riskiest assumption — the one whose failure is fatal? Write it down explicitly.
- What are the three most *likely* (not black-swan) ways this fails?
- Is there a scenario where success itself creates the failure (copycats, backlash, regulatory attention)?

**8. Second-order & systemic effects** *(startups, strategy, policy, broad research)*
- What incentives does this create that don't exist today? (New marketplaces create new fraud.)
- Who gets disintermediated or threatened — and how do they fight back?
- What does widespread adoption look like, and is that world better or worse?

**9. Ethics / externalities / harm** *(all, esp. tech, policy, research)*
- Who bears a cost or risk they didn't consent to?
- What happens when someone with the worst intentions uses this?
- Does this concentrate power, data, or resources in ways that could be abused?

**10. Scale & evolution** *(startups, research, strategy, policy)*
- What breaks first at 10x? 100x?
- What's the end state — the thing this is trying to *become*?
- Is this a feature, a product, or a company? (Or: an insight, an essay, a movement?) Category mistakes are common.

**11. Scope & framing** *(all, esp. early-stage)*
- Is this too big (boiling the ocean) or too small (optimizing a symptom)?
- What's the smallest version that would still be *meaningful* (the irreducible kernel, not a half-baked MVP)?
- What's the real underlying goal? Often the stated idea is one solution to an unstated problem — is it the best one?

**12. Evidence & validation** *(all — the most underused in early thinking)*
- What's the cheapest, fastest experiment that would tell you the core assumption is *wrong*?
- What evidence would make you abandon or change this? (If nothing would, it's an identity, not an idea.)
- Are you seeking disconfirming data as hard as confirming data?

**13. Essays & arguments** *(essays, arguments, advocacy)*
- What's the load-bearing claim — the one assertion that, if wrong, collapses the whole thing?
- What's the strongest counterargument, in its most charitable form?
- Who's the actual target audience — and is it the people who *don't* already agree?
- What's the single thing you want the reader to remember?

## The highest-value blind spots (where great probing pays off most)

1. **The "do nothing" alternative** (D6) — almost no one seriously weighs it; it often wins on switching cost.
2. **The riskiest assumption, made explicit** (D7) — everyone knows there are risks; few can name the fatal one.
3. **Second-order incentives** (D8) — builders optimize the primary behavior, never ask what else they're incentivizing.
4. **Why now** (D5) — the right idea at the wrong time fails.
5. **The non-obvious user** (D2) — the first real adopter is rarely the intended one.
6. **The cheapest falsifiable test** (D12) — structurally underasked because it risks discovering you're wrong.
7. **The endgame** (D10) — ideas without a named destination accumulate locally-reasonable, globally-incoherent decisions.
8. **Power / distribution concentration** (D9) — what regulators, journalists, and adversaries attack first.

## How to choose which dimensions to press

1. **Identify the idea type** and weight accordingly:
   - Startup → 1, 5, 7, 8, 12 · Feature → 1, 2, 3, 6, 11 · Essay → 13, 2, 3, 11
   - Research → 1, 4, 8, 10, 12 · Strategy → 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 · Personal decision → 2, 3, 9, 10, 11
2. **Find what's conspicuously absent** from how they described it (see the SKILL.md signature-move list).
3. **Always probe the load-bearing assumption** at least once, whatever the type.
4. **One dimension deep (2-3 questions), not all dimensions shallow.**
5. **Skip the already-well-considered.** Value is added at the edges of their thinking.
