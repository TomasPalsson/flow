# Development Frameworks — generative moves

**Deliver these as good questions, not named exercises.** The user should feel a sharp probe, not "now we run SCAMPER." Pick by what the idea *needs* (table at the end). Apply 2-3 seriously in a session, not five superficially.

## Sequencing principle: diverge before you converge
Most people (and default AIs) evaluate before they've explored. When a user brings ONE idea and wants to refine it, often the strongest move is to generate alternatives first: *"Before we develop this, what are two completely different approaches to the same problem?"* Comparison reveals hidden assumptions and usually sharpens the original. Force divergence even when they want to evaluate. **The MVP combo for a short session: Jobs-to-be-Done + success premortem.**

## The toolkit

**Jobs-to-be-Done** — *use when building anything for a user; to find what the idea is really for.*
People "hire" a solution to do a functional + emotional + social job. Don't ask "what do you want?" — ask "when you do this task, what matters most? When does it go wrong?" Then the hiring/firing frame: *"What is someone using today, and what would make them fire it for this?"* The real job is usually one or two levels deeper than the surface ("not a drill → a hole → a shelf → a sense of home"). Reframes what the idea *is*.

**Success premortem** — *use after initial articulation, for ambition + the real bet.*
"It's two years out and this succeeded beyond expectations. Walk me through what happened." Then chase the causal chain backward: "what had to happen 6 months before that? And before that?" The final question — *"the single thing that, looking back, made the difference"* — usually surfaces the idea's core bet (what they believe is uniquely true or achievable). More generative than the failure premortem for early ideas.

**Failure premortem** — *use to surface risk as a development agenda, not a kill list.*
"It's a year out and this failed — spectacularly. Why?" Assume failure as *fact* (not "might fail") — narrative reconstruction beats probabilistic forecasting and bypasses optimism bias. Then convert each failure into a build: "if we knew this was coming, what would we build differently?" The premortem becomes a product spec generator.

**Inversion** — *use to find what the idea actually needs / the fatal flaws.*
"How would you *guarantee* this fails — on purpose?" "Guaranteed failures you'd engineer" produces far more specific output than "risks." Generate 10-15, across: kill the core assumption, kill distribution, kill the team, kill the timing, kill the economics. Then per item: "is this path open? what closes it, at what cost?" Often the fastest route to the genuinely fatal flaws.

**Constraint-injection** — *use when there's too much solution space / it needs focus.*
Counterintuitively, a *tight* constraint generates more creative solutions than freedom. "What if you had only $1,000? Only two weeks? One sentence to explain it? No UI?" Tight constraints eliminate the obvious solution space and force new territory. Rule: pick a constraint that blocks the *obvious* path but not all paths.

**Constraint-relaxation** — *use when the idea seems blocked by something.*
"What would you do if [constraint] didn't exist?" Reveals which blockers are real (physics, economics) vs. inherited convention. "What if you didn't need VC funding?" → surfaces bootstrapping paths.

**10x / moonshot** — *use when the idea is stuck in incrementalism ("X but better").*
"What's the version that's 10x bigger, not 10% better?" A 10x target makes iteration impossible and reinvention necessary — forcing first-principles thinking. 10x goals often produce *more* achievable ideas because they land in less-crowded solution space. (Watch the trap: don't just attach bigger numbers to incremental ideas — the cognitive shift to reinvention must actually happen.)

**First-principles** — *use when the idea is trapped by "that's how it's done."*
(1) List the assumed constraints. (2) Sort each: law of physics? regulation? convention? habit? fear? (3) Rebuild using only the real ones. "What's the one thing we must get right for anything else to matter?" Powerful for startups/strategy; weak for genre-bound creative work.

**SCAMPER** — *use when an idea exists but feels stuck/incremental (best for products/services with clear components).*
Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify/Minify, Put-to-other-uses, Eliminate, Reverse. E.g., coffee subscription → *Reverse*: roasters subscribe to find customers. Don't use on spark-stage ideas — nothing to act on yet.

**Lateral thinking (provocation / random entry)** — *use when variations feel too incremental, or thinking is stuck in one frame.*
A deliberate absurd "PO" statement as a stepping stone ("PO: banks pay you to borrow" → reward responsible behavior). Or take an unrelated word, list its properties, force-connect to the problem. Requires willingness to play.

**Morphological analysis (Zwicky box)** — *use when the idea has several independent dimensions to explore systematically.*
Rows = dimensions, columns = options; explore non-obvious *combinations*. (Education platform: Text + Peer + Adaptive + Portfolio + Employer-pays = a genuinely different product than Coursera.) Don't use when dimensions are highly interdependent.

**Analogical / biomimetic transfer** — *use when the idea needs a novel mechanism that doesn't exist in its domain.*
Not "be inspired by nature." (1) Extract the *structural relationship* in your problem. (2) Find a domain where that same relationship is *solved*. (3) Identify the mechanism. (4) Back-translate. (Shinkansen nose ← kingfisher beak ← "fast transition between media without turbulence.") Skip step 1 and it's just aesthetics.

## Pick by what the idea needs

| The idea needs… | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Expansion (too narrow) | Adjacent possibilities, SCAMPER, lateral thinking |
| Problem clarity | JTBD, first-principles |
| Differentiation | Assumption-reversal, 10x, morphological |
| A validation path | Failure premortem, "cheapest falsifiable test" |
| To break a trade-off | Constraint-relaxation, contradiction-breaking |
| A creative leap | Biomimetic/analogical transfer, lateral thinking |
| Ambition + the real bet | 10x, success premortem |

## Pick by stage
- **Spark (just a feeling):** Lateral thinking, JTBD to find the job. Don't apply structured frameworks to a feeling.
- **Rough concept ("X for Y"):** adjacent possibilities, assumption mapping.
- **Structured hypothesis:** premortem, morphological, contradiction-breaking.
- **Refined but incremental:** 10x, assumption-reversal, constraint-injection.
