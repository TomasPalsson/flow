# Second-Level Questions — Marks Discipline

Howard Marks: "First-level thinking says 'I think this is good — let's buy.' Second-level thinking says 'I think this is good, but the consensus also thinks it's good, and the price reflects that, so what's actually undiscounted here?'"

For every recommended pick, the thesis memo must answer Question #1 (the Steinhardt question). The remaining 19 are sanity checks the skill applies during Stage 5.

## The Core Question (mandatory answer in thesis memo)

**1. What does the market believe about this stock that I think is wrong?**
   - Articulate consensus in 1-2 sentences
   - Articulate variant view in 1-2 sentences
   - Why is consensus wrong (or what is being missed)?
   - If you cannot answer → no variant perception → no edge → reject

## Cycle and Regime Awareness

**2. Where are we in the cycle? Does this stock benefit or suffer from late-cycle dynamics?**

**3. Have prices already reflected the macro thesis? (e.g., if Energy is "cheap" but everyone says so, is there still alpha?)**

**4. Is the cycle stage AGAINST this stock? Cyclicals at peak look cheap; compounders at trough look expensive — both signals can mislead.**

## Risk Framing

**5. What's the WORST case? Not the bear case from my model — the genuinely worst case (fraud, regulator action, catastrophic disruption).**

**6. What does a -50% drawdown scenario look like, and would I add or panic-sell?**

**7. Am I confusing volatility with risk? Could a high-volatility name with strong fundamentals be SAFER than a low-volatility name with deteriorating fundamentals?**

## Consensus Decomposition

**8. What is the analyst consensus on near-term earnings? Am I above or below? Why?**

**9. What's the implied 10-year CAGR in current price (reverse DCF)? Is this in top decile of historical base rates? (Mauboussin sanity check)**

**10. Who is the marginal buyer here? Who is the marginal seller? What would change their mind?**

## Margin of Safety

**11. Where is my margin of safety? Against bear case or against base case? Klarman explicitly designed MOS to protect against bear, not base.**

**12. What's the asymmetry ratio? Required ≥ 2:1. Why specifically?**

**13. What would I have to be wrong about for this to lose 30%? Is it specific (one variable) or general (whole thesis)?**

## Quality of Knowledge

**14. What do I know that the market doesn't? Or is my edge purely interpretation?**

**15. Am I within my circle of competence? Predictability test: "Can I judge that this business will look a lot like it does today in 5 years?" (Buffett's exact phrasing)**

**16. What evidence would change my mind? (Pre-commit to the falsifying observation.)**

## Time Frame and Patience

**17. Am I sized correctly to hold through a 30-40% drawdown for 2 years? Or am I implicitly assuming the catalyst plays out fast?**

**18. What's the optionality value of waiting? Is there a better entry in the next 6-12 months?**

## Recursive Second-Level

**19. Have I considered that the consensus has ALSO done the analysis I just did? If my "variant perception" is the standard Buffett/Lynch reading, it's probably priced in. What's the second-order variant?**

**20. Am I right for the right reasons? Or am I going to be lucky-wrong (right answer, wrong reasoning)?**

## Application During Stage 5

During Stage 5 deep-dive, work through questions 1-20 in order. For each:
- If answer is unclear → flag, don't auto-fail
- If 5+ are weakly answered → reduce CS100 confidence, expand dispersion to ±15
- If Q1 (variant perception) cannot be answered → reject the candidate
- If Q15 (circle of competence) fails → reject (can't evaluate)
- If Q5/Q6 (worst case + drawdown tolerance) fails → reduce position size to 1/4-Kelly

## Anti-Patterns

- **Going through the motions** — answering "yes" to all 20 without genuine engagement defeats the purpose
- **Confirmation in disguise** — restating the bull thesis as "second-level"
- **False contrarianism** — being negative just to seem clever is not second-level
- **Recursion paralysis** — Q19 can be applied infinitely; one level is sufficient

## Marks Quote (memo at exit)

"To be a successful contrarian, you have to be willing to be wrong for a while. That is, the contrarian view often looks like — and turns out to be — wrong in the short term. The proof that you're a thoughtful contrarian rather than a stubborn one is that you are right in the long term."

This is a frame for HOLDING discipline once the pick is made — the conviction must survive interim adversity if the thesis is sound.
